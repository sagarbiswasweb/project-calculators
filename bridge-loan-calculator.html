How to Use the Bridge Loan Calculator for Accurate Loan Estimates

The bridge loan calculator is easy to use. Here’s a step-by-step guide:

Loan A: Bridge Loan on Departure Property

  1. Current Market Value: Enter your current property's market value in this field.
  2. Loan to Value: Input here the percentage, which determines the maximum amount you can borrow against the value of your current property.
  3. Outstanding Mortgage: Enter the remaining mortgage balance on your current property.
  4. Closing Costs: Enter the Closing Costs, which include expenses associated with selling your existing property or securing the loan.

Click the "Calculate" button to view the bridge loan amount available for your departure property.

If you wish to reset the inputs, click the "Clear" button.

Loan B: New Property Purchase Loan

  1. Purchase Price : Enter the Purchase Price of the new property you plan to buy in the first field.
  2. Cash from Liquid Assets Used for Down Payment: Input the Cash from Liquid Assets you plan to use as a down payment for the new property.

Click the "Calculate" button to determine the loan amount needed to purchase the new property.

To reset the input fields, use the "Clear" button.

Understanding Your Results

The Bridge Loan Calculator provides key outputs that help you make informed decisions when transitioning between properties. Below is an explanation of the results displayed in Loan A and Loan B sections.

Loan A: Bridge Loan on Departure Property

After entering the required inputs and clicking Calculate, the results will show the following:

  1. Available Cash Out for Down Payment:
    • This is the amount of cash you can use from the equity of your current property after subtracting the outstanding mortgage and closing costs from the bridge loan amount.
    • It represents the portion of funds you can allocate toward the purchase of a new property.
  2. Bridge Loan Amount:
    • This is the total loan amount available for your current property based on the entered Loan-to-Value (LTV) percentage and the market value of the property.

Loan B: New Property Purchase Loan

After completing this section and clicking Calculate, the following results are displayed:

  1. Available Cash from Bridge Loan:
    • This value is carried over from the Loan A section and indicates the amount of cash available for the down payment on the new property.
  2. New Property Loan Amount:
    • This is the total loan amount required to purchase the new property after subtracting the cash from liquid assets and bridge loan funds from the purchase price.
  3. New Property Loan-to-Value (LTV):
    • This is the ratio of the new property loan amount to the purchase price, expressed as a percentage. It helps you evaluate whether the loan aligns with typical lending requirements.

What is a Bridge Loan?

A bridge loan is a short-term financing solution that helps borrowers cover immediate expenses while waiting for long-term financing. It acts as a financial bridge, providing quick access to funds for homeowners, real estate investors, and businesses.

Typically, bridge loans range from 6 months to 3 years and are secured by the borrower's property. They are commonly used to finance real estate transactions, cover down payments, or manage temporary cash flow gaps.

  1. Short-Term Duration:

    Most bridge loans have a repayment period between 6 months and 3 years, making them a temporary financing option.

  2. Secured by Property:

    These loans use your property's equity as collateral, ensuring lender security while providing fast access to funds.

  3. Flexible Usage:

    Bridge loans help cover down payments, closing costs, and property renovations, making them ideal for buyers in transition.

  4. Higher Interest Rates:

    Due to their short-term nature and fast approval, bridge loan interest rates tend to be higher than conventional mortgage rates.

What Affects the Cost of a Bridge Loan?

There are different things involved in the cost of a bridge loan that you need to know before using any complicated bridge loan calculator.

  • Interest Rates: Bridge loans have higher interest rates than typical bank lending. Rates are subject to change and may vary by creditworthiness.
  • Loan Terms: The longer your loan term is the less you'll pay each month but more in interest over time and vice versa for shorter terms. On the downside, longer terms mean smaller monthly payments and larger lifetime interest costs.
  • Fees: Fees, fees, and more fees – there will be origination fees or closing costs or both. Those costs are usually a percent of the loan amount.

Ways To Save Money On Bridge Loans

Lowering the costs of bridge loans makes them a more practicable solution.

  • Improve Your Credit Score: A better credit score will help you secure a lower interest rate, which ultimately directly reduces the total expense.
  • Shop Around: To find the best deal, compare with other lenders. Do not settle with the very first parcel that is offered to you.
  • Negotiate Fees: But some lenders might be more lenient with other charges, like those that can primarily affect low-income borrowers. It never hurts to ask.
  • Financing Your Bridge Loan: Knowing how you are going to pay off your bridge loan can save money and hassle.
  • Personal Savings: If you have some savings, using personal funds can decrease the sum that needs to be borrowed and reduce your interest expense.
  • Other Loans: Some banks will allow you to take out an additional financing in other terms such as a HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit) for the down payment, so that large upfront payment does not have to be put towards your bridge loan.
